## Section: Machine Learning

> Machine learning itself is **not something you need to know in depth** for the exam, but you **must know what each of these services does**.

### Lecture: Rekognition Overview

- **Amazon Rekognition** **recognizes objects, people, text and scenes in images and videos** using machine learning.
- Supports **facial analysis** and **facial search** for **user verification** and **counting people**.
- You can build a **database of familiar faces** or **compare against celebrities**.

**Use cases**
- **Labeling** — identify what is in an image (e.g., person, rock, mountain bike, crest, outdoors; Golden Retriever / dog).
- **Content moderation** — verify content is appropriate for all ages.
- **Text detection** — e.g., read the race numbers of runners in a photo.
- **Face detection and analysis** — gender, **age range**, **emotions** (e.g., "this person looks happy, smiling, eyes open, female").
- **Face search and verification** — e.g., a security application.
- **Celebrity recognition.**
- **Pathing** — e.g., monitoring a soccer game to see where everyone moves, for real-time analytics.

### Lecture: Transcribe Overview

- **Amazon Transcribe** **automatically converts speech into text**.
- Uses a deep learning process called **ASR (Automatic Speech Recognition)** to convert speech to text quickly and accurately.

**Features to know**
- **Automatically remove PII (Personally Identifiable Information) using redaction** — e.g., age, name, Social Security Number can be hidden automatically.
- **Automatic language identification for multilingual audio** — it can recognize French, English, Spanish, etc. in the same audio.

**Use cases**
- Transcribe **customer service calls**.
- Automate **closed captioning and subtitling**.
- **Generate metadata for media assets** to create a **fully searchable archive**.

**Hands-on notes**
- Choose the language (e.g., English US) and **start streaming** — spoken audio is transcribed into text live.
- Enabling **PII identification and redaction** hides the selected categories (e.g., name and phone number are masked in the output).
- Enabling **automatic language identification** with multiple languages selected (e.g., English + French) lets it transcribe both within one stream.

---

### Lecture: Polly Overview

- **Amazon Polly is the opposite of Transcribe**: it turns **text into speech** using **deep learning**.
- Lets you build **applications that talk** — you supply text and Polly generates playable audio.
- In the console you enter text and press **Listen**. Choosing the **standard engine** produces a more **robot-sounding** voice (versus the more natural default).

---

### Lecture: Translate Overview

- **Amazon Translate** provides **natural and accurate language translation**.
- Lets you **localize content** — websites and applications — for **international users**, and **translate large volumes of text efficiently**.
- Example: "Hi, my name is Stephane" translated into **French, Portuguese and Hindi**.

---

### Lecture: Lex + Connect Overview

**Amazon Lex**
- The **same technology that powers Alexa** devices (the home device you ask "Alexa, what's the weather like tomorrow?").
- Provides **ASR (Automatic Speech Recognition)** — converts **spoken words into text**.
- Understands the **intent** of the text using **natural language understanding** — it understands whole sentences.
- Used to build **chatbots** and **call center bots**.

**Amazon Connect**
- A **visual contact center**, fully **cloud-based**: receive calls, create **contact flows**.
- **Integrates** with other **CRM** (customer relationship management) systems and AWS services.
- Advantages vs. traditional contact centers: **no upfront payment** and about **80% cheaper**.

**Combined flow (smart contact center)**
1. A customer calls a number defined by **Amazon Connect** (e.g., to schedule an appointment).
2. **Lex** streams the call, converts speech to text and **understands the intent**.
3. Lex **invokes the right Lambda function**, which (with your code) acts on the intent — e.g., "schedule a meeting tomorrow with Tom at 3:00 PM" → write it into the CRM.

- > **Exam Tip**: remember **Lex = ASR / chatbots**, **Connect = contact center**.

### Lecture: Comprehend Overview

- **Amazon Comprehend** is for **NLP (Natural Language Processing)**.
- > **Exam Tip**: anytime you see **NLP** on the exam, think **Amazon Comprehend**.
- **Fully managed and serverless**; uses machine learning to **find insights and relationships in text**.

**What it can do**
- Detect the **language** of the text.
- Extract **key phrases, places, people, brands, events**.
- **Sentiment analysis** — how **positive or negative** the text is.
- Analyze text using **tokenization and parts of speech**.
- **Organize a collection of text files by topic**, discovering the topics itself.
- In short: takes **unstructured text data** in and **structures** it around these features.

**Use cases**
- **Analyze customer interactions** — e.g., feed in customer support emails to understand what leads to a **positive or negative customer experience**, extract those features and gain **business insight** to improve the business.
- **Group articles by topic** — feed in many articles and Comprehend outputs the topics to group them by, instead of reading them one by one.

### Lecture: SageMaker AI Overview

- **Amazon SageMaker** is a **fully managed service for developers and data scientists to build machine learning models**.
- **Difference from the other services in this section**: all the others are **managed ML services with a very specific purpose** (translate text, transcribe audio, text-to-speech, analyze text). SageMaker is a **higher-level, general ML service** where **your own developers/data scientists create and build models** — it is **much more involved and much harder to use**.
- Building a model normally requires **many difficult steps** plus **provisioning servers** for the computations — cumbersome. SageMaker helps **all along the way**.

**The ML process (simplified example: predicting a student's exam score)**
1. **Gather data** — e.g., survey 10,000 students on years of IT experience, years of AWS experience, time spent on the course, number of practice exams taken.
2. **Label the data** — define what each column corresponds to and attach the **outcome/score** (e.g., 670 = did not pass, 990, 934). Labeling can be **quite complicated in practice**.
3. **Build the machine learning model** — how to predict the score from historical data.
4. **Train and tune** the model — refining it over time to better fit the data and outputs; also **quite difficult**.
5. **Deploy the model** and use it — new data comes in (a new student's answers), the model is applied, and it **predicts** the outcome (e.g., "this student will pass with a score of 906").
- **All of these steps — labeling, building, training, tuning and applying — can be done within SageMaker.**

### Lecture: Kendra Overview

- **Amazon Kendra** is a **fully managed document search service powered by machine learning**.
- **Extracts answers from within a document** — documents can be **text, PDF, HTML, PowerPoint, Microsoft Word, FAQs**, etc., from many data sources.
- Kendra **indexes** the documents and builds an internal **knowledge index** powered by machine learning.

**End-user capabilities**
- **Natural language search** — like using Google. Example: a user asks *"Where is the IT support desk?"* and Kendra answers *"1st floor"*, because it learned that from the indexed documents.
- **Incremental learning** — learns from **user interactions and feedback** to promote **preferred search results**.
- **Fine-tune search results** — based on **importance of data**, **freshness**, or any **custom filters**.
- > **Exam Tip**: whenever you see a **document search service**, think **Amazon Kendra**.

---

### Lecture: Personalize Overview

- **Amazon Personalize** is a fully managed machine learning service to build apps with **real-time personalized recommendations**.
- Types of output: **personalized product recommendations**, **re-ranking**, **customized direct marketing** (e.g., a user who bought many gardening tools gets recommendations for the next one to buy).
- **Same technology used by Amazon.com** — after you buy a few products, Amazon.com recommends products in the same or different categories based on your searching, buying and interests.

**Integrations**
- **Input data** read from **Amazon S3** (e.g., user interactions).
- **Amazon Personalize API** for **real-time data integration**.
- Exposes a **customized personalized API** for your **websites, applications and mobile applications**.
- Can also drive **SMS or email** personalization.
- **Takes days, not months**, to build the model — you **don't need to build, train and deploy** ML solutions yourself; use it as bundled.
- **Use cases**: **retail stores**, **media and entertainment**.
- > **Exam Tip**: a machine learning service to build **(personalized) recommendations** → **Amazon Personalize**.

---

### Lecture: Textract Overview

- **Amazon Textract** **extracts text, handwriting and data from any scanned document**, using AI/machine learning behind the scenes.
- Example: upload a **driver's license** → it is analyzed and results are returned as a **data file** from which you can extract the **date of birth, document ID**, etc.
- Can extract data even from **forms and tables**, and can read **PDFs, images**, etc.

**Use cases**
- **Financial services** — process **invoices and financial reports**.
- **Healthcare** — **medical records** and **insurance claims**.
- **Public sector** — **tax forms, ID documents, passports**.

## Section: Machine Learning — Key Takeaways

**Most important concepts**
- AWS's ML portfolio splits into **purpose-built, ready-to-use managed services** (Rekognition, Transcribe, Polly, Translate, Lex, Connect, Comprehend, Kendra, Personalize, Textract) and **one general-purpose platform** (**SageMaker**) where your own developers/data scientists build, train, tune and deploy custom models.
- The exam tests **service-to-use-case mapping**, not ML theory. Each service has one signature keyword: Rekognition = **images/video**, Transcribe = **speech→text**, Polly = **text→speech**, Translate = **translation**, Lex = **chatbot/ASR**, Connect = **contact center**, Comprehend = **NLP**, Kendra = **document search**, Personalize = **recommendations**, Textract = **extract text from documents**, SageMaker = **build your own model**.
- The **ML lifecycle** (gather data → **label** → **build** → **train and tune** → **deploy/apply**) is the reason SageMaker exists: each step is hard, and it also removes the need to provision compute servers for training.
- Two services are explicit opposites: **Transcribe (speech → text)** and **Polly (text → speech)**.
- Several services are designed to be combined: **Lex + Connect** builds a smart cloud contact center, with **Lambda** executing the business action once Lex determines the caller's intent.

**How the concepts relate**
- **Transcribe → Comprehend** is a natural chain: convert calls to text, then run NLP/sentiment analysis on that text to derive business insight.
- **Lex** provides the ASR and intent understanding that makes **Connect** a *smart* contact center; **Lambda** performs the resulting work (e.g., writing to a CRM) — reusing serverless concepts from earlier sections.
- **Personalize** and **Kendra** both consume your existing data (S3 input data, indexed documents) and expose an **API** your applications call — the ML model is managed for you, unlike SageMaker.
- **Textract vs. Rekognition text detection**: both read text, but Textract targets **scanned documents, forms and tables**, while Rekognition targets **text within images and video scenes**.
- Privacy features appear across services — **Transcribe PII redaction** parallels **Macie's** PII discovery from the Security section.

**Key terminology to remember**
- ASR (Automatic Speech Recognition), NLP (Natural Language Processing), deep learning
- PII redaction, automatic language identification
- Labeling, content moderation, face detection/analysis, face search and verification, celebrity recognition, pathing
- Sentiment analysis, key phrases, tokenization, parts of speech, topic modeling
- Knowledge index, natural language search, incremental learning
- Personalized recommendations, re-ranking, customized direct marketing
- Model labeling → building → training → tuning → deployment; data scientist
- Contact flows, CRM, chatbot / call center bot

**Key distinctions/comparisons**
- **Transcribe vs. Polly**: speech → text vs. text → speech.
- **Purpose-built AI services vs. SageMaker**: pre-built, easy, one specific task, no model building vs. fully featured platform for developers/data scientists, much more involved and difficult.
- **Lex vs. Connect**: speech recognition and intent understanding (chatbots) vs. the cloud contact center that receives the calls and runs contact flows.
- **Comprehend vs. Kendra**: understand and structure text (language, entities, sentiment, topics) vs. **search** documents and return **answers** to natural-language questions.
- **Kendra vs. Personalize**: ML-powered document search vs. ML-powered recommendations.
- **Textract vs. Rekognition**: extract text/handwriting/data from **scanned documents, forms and tables** vs. recognize objects, people, scenes and text in **images and videos**.
- **Amazon Connect vs. traditional contact centers**: no upfront payment, ~80% cheaper, cloud-based vs. traditional on-premises offerings.
- **Polly standard engine vs. default**: more robot-sounding voice vs. more natural voice.

**You should be able to explain:**
- What each of the eleven services in this section does, and pick the right one for a described scenario.
- Which service converts speech to text, which does the reverse, and which handles translation.
- What ASR and NLP mean, and which services provide them.
- How Lex and Connect combine (with Lambda) to build a smart cloud contact center, and Connect's cost advantages.
- Why SageMaker is different in kind from the other ML services, and the five stages of the ML process it supports.
- What Rekognition's main use cases are (labeling, content moderation, text detection, face analysis, face search, celebrity recognition, pathing).
- Which service to use for document search (Kendra), recommendations (Personalize), sensitive-data-free transcription (Transcribe with PII redaction), and document data extraction (Textract).
