### Lecture: Words on Other Services

- **Distractors**: you will see services in the answer options that **were never covered in the course**.
- There are **over 200 AWS services**; covering them all would be overwhelming and meaningless. Examples of services that may appear as distractors: **QuickSight, Cognito, AppStream, Server Migration Service**, etc.
- The course intentionally covers only the services the instructor's research and experience indicate you will actually be **questioned on** — **more than 40 services**.
- **Distractors should never be the correct answer.** Seeing an unfamiliar service in a question is **normal** — don't panic.
- If you genuinely see an **uncovered service as a correct answer at the exam**, report it so the course can be updated. The goal is **not 700/1000 but over 900/1000**, so even one uncovered question will not stop you from passing.

### Lecture: State of Learning Checkpoint - AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner

**The official exam guide (CLF-C02)**
- States what is expected of you: **explain the value of the cloud**, understand **security best practices**, the **shared responsibility model**, **cloud concepts**, **core services**, and the **economics of the AWS cloud**.
- It suggests **up to six months of exposure to AWS Cloud design**; the course provides the necessary knowledge regardless.

**Question types**
- **Multiple choice** — **one correct answer** and **three incorrect responses** (the incorrect ones are called **distractors**).
- **Multiple response** — **two or more correct responses out of five or more options**; the question **tells you how many** responses to select.

**Scoring rules**
- **An unanswered question is marked incorrect.**
- **There is no penalty for guessing** → **answer every single question**.
- **65 questions total: 50 affect your score, 15 are unscored** (used by AWS to collect data on whether a question is good).
- The exam is scored from **100 to 1000**; the **passing score is 700**.

**Domain weighting**

| Domain | Weight |
|---|---|
| **Cloud Concepts** | **24%** |
| **Security and Compliance** | **30%** |
| **Cloud Technology and Services** | **34%** |
| **Billing, Pricing and Support** | **12%** |

- The exam guide is specific about what each domain covers, and also contains an **appendix on technologies and concepts** — a list of services that **may or may not appear** at the exam. Services in that appendix but **absent from the course are intentional omissions** (likely distractors only).
- **Official prep question set** is available free on **AWS Skill Builder** — **20 questions** to practice and understand how the exam is structured.

### Lecture: Exam Sample Question Walkthrough

Worked examples showing how questions are structured and how to reason through them:

**1. "Automatically discover, classify, and protect sensitive data stored in Amazon S3."** — Options: Inspector, **Macie**, GuardDuty, Secrets Manager.
- **Inspector** → inspects **EC2 instances** mainly.
- **Macie** → **protects sensitive data in Amazon S3** ✅
- **GuardDuty** → automatic **account protection**.
- **Secrets Manager** → **stores secrets**.
- *Pattern*: most questions are a **definition → choose a service**.

**2. "Which service allows customers to purchase unused EC2 capacity at an often-discounted rate?"** — Options: Reserved Instances, On-Demand, Dedicated, **Spot Instances**.
- **Reserved** → when you know you want an instance for **1 or 3 years**.
- **On-Demand** → the **default** mode; terminate whenever you want.
- **Dedicated** → **dedicated hardware**.
- **Spot Instances** → **purchase unused EC2 capacity** ✅

**3. "A company hosts a static website from a single Amazon S3 bucket. Which service achieves lower latency and high transfer speeds?"** — Options: Elastic Beanstalk, DynamoDB Accelerator (DAX), Route 53, **Amazon CloudFront**.
- **Beanstalk** → launch Java/Node.js applications through one service.
- **DAX** → accelerates **DynamoDB**.
- **Route 53** → networking and **DNS**.
- **CloudFront** → used with S3 to achieve **lower latency and high transfer speeds** by **caching** S3 data ✅

**4. "A company wants a solution to store and manage their developer source code."** — Options: CodeArtifact, CodeBuild, CodePipeline, **CodeCommit** ✅
- *Tip*: **you only need to know the right answer, not necessarily what the other options are.**

**5. "What are the advantages of deploying an application with Amazon EC2 in multiple Availability Zones? (Select TWO)"**
- ✅ **Preventing a single point of failure.**
- ✅ **Increasing the availability of the application** (this is why it's called **high availability**).
- ❌ Reducing cost — you are now using **more** Availability Zones.
- ❌ Serving cross-region users with low latency — **Availability Zones are within the same region**.
- ❌ Increasing the load of the application — you actually **spread** the load across zones.

**6. "An on-premises Linux-based server runs an Oracle database. The company wants to migrate the database server to run on an Amazon EC2 instance. Which service completes the migration?"** — Options: Database Migration Service (DMS), Migration Hub, **Application Migration Service (MGN)**, Application Discovery Service.
- **Migration Hub** → oversees migrations overall.
- **Application Discovery Service** → **discovers** applications to migrate.
- Two options look plausible (**DMS** and **MGN**), but the correct answer is **Application Migration Service (MGN)** ✅
- **Why**: **DMS migrates a database to a database service** (e.g., to RDS). Here the **target is an EC2 instance**, so what you are really migrating is a **Linux-based server** — it does not matter that the server happens to run Oracle. **A slightly tricky question — read the target carefully.**

### Lecture: Exam Tips - AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner

**Preparation**
- **Practice makes perfect** — follow the hands-on in the course if you are new to AWS; **don't rush** to the exam, make sure you understand what services do and how they work.
- The exam recommends **six months or more of hands-on experience**; doing the course hands-on builds a lot of that experience.
- If you feel overwhelmed by the volume (**40+ services in ~10 hours**), **go through the course a second time** to solidify the concepts.

**Question types and mechanics**
- **Multiple choice**: **one correct** answer, **three incorrect**; shown with **radio buttons** (only one selectable).
- **Multiple response**: **two or more correct** out of **five or more** options.
- > **Important**: the **exam software does NOT check whether you selected the right number of answers**. If a question says "select two", you must select **exactly two** — the software will not complain if you select one or three. **Be careful to tick the number of boxes the question asks for.**
- **Always answer every question** — unanswered = incorrect; **wrong answers are not penalized**, so **guess** if needed.
- **Flag questions for review**: you can flag a question and return to it. After answering everything, a **flag screen** lets you jump back to flagged (or any other) questions to answer or double-check them.

**Answering strategy**
- **Proceed by elimination.** Most questions are **high-level "pick a service"** questions — rule out the answers you know are wrong, then choose the most sensible remaining option.
- There are **very few trick questions** at the Cloud Practitioner and Associate levels — **don't overthink it**.
- **If a solution seems feasible but highly complicated, it is probably wrong.** **Think simple and follow your intuition.**

**Extra study**
- **Read each service's overview page** (e.g., the S3 overview) — these overviews represent a lot of what the exam asks and confirm your understanding.
- **Join the AWS community** — answer other people's questions in the course Q&A to confirm your own understanding; read forums and blogs; attend local meetups; watch **re:Invent** videos on YouTube (re:Invent is AWS's conference).
- Take the **practice exam in this section**, and additional practice exams if you want more.

**Exam logistics**
- **Register online**; the **fee is $100 USD**.
- You must provide **two identity documents** (e.g., ID and credit card); details arrive by email after registration.
- **Not allowed**: taking notes, using a pen, or speaking.
- **65 questions in 90 minutes.**
- You can optionally **review all questions and answers** at the end, and **flag difficult questions** along the way to revisit.
- **On submission you immediately learn pass or fail** ("Congratulations, pass" or "Fail"). **You will not learn which answers were right or wrong** — this is intended.
- Your **overall score arrives by email a few days later** — normally **2 days**, sometimes up to **5 days**.
- **Passing score: 700 out of 1000.**
- **If you fail, you can retake the exam after 14 days.**

### Lecture: Exam Walkthrough and Signup

- Go to **aws.training/certification** → **Sign In** → create an **AWS Builder ID** (a specific account for AWS certifications) → **Go to Your Account** to reach the **certification portal**.
- **Exam Registration → Schedule an exam** lists the exams you can take. Some show **"click to authorize to schedule"** — click **Authorize** first, then **Schedule** becomes available.
- Scheduling takes you to the **Pearson VUE** portal (AWS's exam scheduling partner), with **two delivery options**:

| In person (test center) | Online (**OnVUE**) |
|---|---|
| Take the exam in a specific room | Take it **from home** |
| Bring your **photo ID**; know what you can and cannot bring | Need a **computer with a webcam** and a **good internet connection** |
| Select **language**, then **location** (agree to conditions, search by address) | **Run the system test well before the exam** to make sure everything works |
| | **Testing space must be clear** — nothing on your desk; the proctor will ask you to show your desk |
| | Bring an **ID**; **check in about 30 minutes before** your appointment |

- Remaining steps: choose your **exam language**, agree to the conditions, choose the **language of the person checking you in** (separate from the exam language), set your **time zone**, pick a **date and time**, **book**, and **complete payment** (a **voucher/discount code** can be added).
- **To take the exam**: from **Home → Dashboard**, click **Manage my Pearson VUE account** → the Pearson VUE homepage shows your exam with a **Launch** button.
- The instructor also recommends watching the next two lectures: **exam accommodations** (extra time for non-native English speakers) and **certification benefits** (50% off your next exam).

## Section: Preparing for the Exam + Practice Exam — Key Takeaways

**Most important concepts**
- **Exam format**: **65 questions in 90 minutes** — **50 scored, 15 unscored**; scored **100–1000** with a **passing score of 700**; **$100 USD** fee; **retake after 14 days** if you fail.
- **Two question types**: **multiple choice** (1 of 4) and **multiple response** (2+ correct out of 5+). The question states how many to select, and the **software does not verify that you selected the right number** — that is your responsibility.
- **Never leave a question unanswered** — blanks count as wrong and **guessing carries no penalty**. Use the **flag** feature to revisit uncertain questions.
- **Domain weights**: **Cloud Technology and Services 34%**, **Security and Compliance 30%**, **Cloud Concepts 24%**, **Billing, Pricing and Support 12%**.
- **Distractors** are deliberately unfamiliar services in the answer options; they are **not the correct answer**. Focus on the **40+ services covered in the course**.
- **Answering strategy**: most questions are **"definition → pick a service"**; **eliminate** wrong options, avoid overthinking, and prefer the **simple** solution — a feasible but highly complicated option is probably wrong.
- **Read the target of the question carefully** — the Oracle-on-Linux example shows that migrating a *server to EC2* is **MGN**, not **DMS**, even though a database is involved.
- **Results**: pass/fail is shown immediately; **which answers were wrong is never revealed**; the numeric score arrives by email in about **2 days (up to 5)**.
- **Logistics**: register at aws.training/certification with an **AWS Builder ID**, schedule via **Pearson VUE**, choose **test center** or **OnVUE at home** (run the **system test** in advance, clear desk, **check in 30 minutes early**, bring **two identity documents**), and no notes/pen/speaking are allowed.
- **Two cost/time levers**: passing an AWS exam grants a **50%-off voucher** for the next one, and **non-native English speakers can claim ESL +30 minutes** (never expires, but must be approved **before** scheduling).

**How the concepts relate**
- The **domain weights explain where to spend study time**: Cloud Technology and Services plus Security and Compliance together are **64%** of the exam, which matches the volume of the earlier sections on core services and on security.
- The **question style (definition → service)** is exactly why the per-service one-line definitions and the "Exam Tip" keyword hooks throughout the course matter more than deep configuration detail.
- **Distractors and the exam guide's appendix are the same phenomenon**: services listed but never taught are there to be recognized and ruled out during **elimination**.
- **No-penalty guessing + flagging + elimination** combine into a single exam tactic: answer everything on the first pass, flag the uncertain ones, then reason by elimination on the review pass.
- The **accommodation and voucher lectures are sequencing-sensitive**: the ESL accommodation must be approved **before** scheduling, and the voucher must be applied **at checkout** — both are process steps, not knowledge.

**Key terminology to remember**
- Distractor, multiple choice vs. multiple response, scored vs. unscored questions, flag for review
- CLF-C02, exam guide, appendix on technologies and concepts, AWS Skill Builder prep question set
- Passing score 700/1000, 65 questions, 90 minutes, 14-day retake window, $100 exam fee
- Exam domains: Cloud Concepts (24%), Security and Compliance (30%), Cloud Technology and Services (34%), Billing/Pricing/Support (12%)
- AWS Builder ID, aws.training/certification, Pearson VUE, OnVUE, system test, exam accommodation (**ESL +30 minutes**), certification benefit voucher (**50% off**)
- re:Invent, course Q&A, service overview pages

**Key distinctions/comparisons**
- **Multiple choice vs. multiple response**: exactly one correct answer (radio buttons) vs. two or more correct answers out of five or more (you must select the stated number).
- **Scored vs. unscored questions**: 50 count toward your score vs. 15 used by AWS for question quality data — you cannot tell them apart, so treat all 65 equally.
- **Unanswered vs. wrong answer**: both count as incorrect, but since **wrong answers carry no penalty**, guessing is strictly better than leaving a blank.
- **Test center vs. OnVUE (online)**: attend a physical room with photo ID and restricted items vs. take it at home needing a webcam, good internet, a pre-run system test and a completely clear desk with 30-minute early check-in.
- **Immediate result vs. detailed score**: pass/fail on submission vs. the numeric score by email in ~2–5 days, with **no per-question feedback ever**.
- **DMS vs. MGN (worked example)**: migrate a **database into a database service** (e.g., RDS) vs. migrate a **server (physical/virtual) into EC2**, regardless of what runs on it.
- **Services covered in the course vs. services in the exam guide appendix**: the ~40 you must know vs. the longer list where extras appear only as distractors.

**You should be able to explain:**
- The exam's format, duration, question counts, scoring range, passing score and retake policy.
- The four exam domains and their weightings, and what that implies for study priorities.
- What a distractor is and why unfamiliar service names in the options should not worry you.
- Why you must answer every question, and how flagging and elimination fit into an exam strategy.
- The two question types and the fact that the software will not warn you about selecting the wrong number of answers.
- How to reason through a typical "definition → service" question, using the Macie, Spot Instances, CloudFront, CodeCommit, multi-AZ and MGN examples.
- How to register, schedule (test center vs. OnVUE), what to prepare on exam day, and what is not allowed.
- How to obtain and apply a 50%-off voucher, and how to request the ESL +30-minute accommodation and the required ordering of steps.
