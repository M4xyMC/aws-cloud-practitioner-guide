## Section: AWS Architecting & Ecosystem

### Lecture: AWS WhitePapers Well-Architected Framework

**General guiding principles for good cloud architecture**
- **Stop guessing your capacity needs** — use **Auto Scaling** and scale based on actual demand.
- **Test systems at production scale** — in the cloud you can create as many resources as you want very quickly, so there is no reason not to test at production scale for an hour; this ensures the system is ready before you publish it to customers.
- **Automate to make architectural experimentation easier** — **CloudFormation** (infrastructure as code) lets you recreate an architecture in different accounts and regions; **PaaS** such as **Elastic Beanstalk** helps you experiment quickly.
- **Allow for evolutionary architectures** — design based on **changing requirements**. When migrating from on-premises you may start with a 1:1 match, then rethink how to leverage the cloud better (e.g., go serverless).
- **Drive architectures using data** — don't guess; look at actual usage, patterns and queries, and choose services based on that data.
- **Improve through game days** — simulate applications for **flash sale days** to stress the system and learn whether you're ready.
  - *Example*: **Netflix's "Chaos Monkey"** randomly **terminates EC2 instances in production** to verify readiness for failures, spikes and errors.

**Design principles / best practices when using the cloud**
- **Scalability** — **vertical** and **horizontal** scalability.
- **Disposable resources** — servers should be **easily disposable and easily configured**. Don't put so much configuration on a server that losing it costs days of rework; **back up data and configuration** and be able to **reconfigure the whole architecture quickly**. In the cloud **everything is disposable, including your infrastructure**.
- **Automation** — guided by serverless, infrastructure as a service, Auto Scaling, etc.
- **Loose coupling** — a **monolith** (one application doing everything) grows harder to maintain and scale. Break it into **smaller loosely coupled components** linked via **SNS, SQS** or other means, so that **a change or failure in one component does not cascade** to the others. Things fail in the cloud — be prepared.
- **Think in services, not servers** — using only EC2 just translates on-premises into the cloud; prefer **managed services** (databases, serverless, …).

**The six pillars of the Well-Architected Framework**
1. **Operational Excellence**
2. **Security**
3. **Reliability**
4. **Performance Efficiency**
5. **Cost Optimization**
6. **Sustainability**

- The pillars are **not trade-offs to balance against each other — they are a synergy**. E.g., improving **operational excellence** usually also improves **cost optimization**.

### Lecture: Pillar 1: Operational Excellence

- **Definition**: the ability to **run and monitor systems to deliver business value** and to **continuously improve supporting processes and procedures**.

**Design principles**
- **Perform operations as code** — infrastructure as code (**AWS CloudFormation**).
- **Make frequent, small, reversible changes** — so failures can be reversed. Huge changes every three months go badly.
- **Refine operations procedures frequently** and ensure all team members are familiar with them.
- **Anticipate failure**, and **learn from all failures** — failing is learning, but only if you take the feedback.
- **Use managed services** to reduce operational burden.
- **Implement observability for actionable insights** — performance, reliability and cost.

**AWS services mapped to the pillar** *(educational only — you don't need to memorize these mappings for the exam)*
- **Prepare**: runbooks, infrastructure standards, mock deployments → **CloudFormation**, **Config** (evaluate compliance of your CloudFormation templates).
- **Operate**: automate as much as possible, release fast, avoid manual processes → **CloudFormation**, **Config**, **CloudTrail** (track API calls, detect manual changes), **CloudWatch** (monitor performance over time), **X-Ray** (trace HTTP requests and pinpoint where things go wrong).
- **Evolve**: **CloudFormation** plus the CI/CD tools **CodeBuild, CodeCommit, CodeDeploy, CodePipeline** — iterate quickly, deploy often, deploy small changes.

### Lecture: Pillar 2: Security

- **Definition**: the ability to **protect information, systems and assets** while delivering business value through **risk assessment and mitigation strategies**.
- A secure application **minimizes risk over time and saves costs from disasters**.

**Design principles**
- **Implement a strong identity foundation** — **centralize user account management**, rely on **least privilege** (**IAM**).
- **Enable traceability** — collect and store **all logs and metrics**, and **automatically respond/take action** when something looks unusual.
- **Apply security at all layers** — edge network, VPC, subnet, load balancer, every EC2 instance, the OS (patching), the application (kept up to date) — so if one layer fails the next takes over.
- **Automate security best practices** — security is done well when automated, not manually.
- **Protect data in transit and at rest** — always enable **encryption**, use **SSL**, **tokenization** and **access control**.
- **Keep people away from data** — question why someone needs direct access; automate away manual data handling.
- **Prepare for security events** — run **response simulations**, use tools to **speed detection, investigation and recovery**.

**AWS services mapped to the pillar**
- **Identity and access management**: **IAM**, **STS** (temporary credentials), **MFA tokens**, **AWS Organizations** (manage multiple accounts centrally).
- **Detective controls**: **Config** (compliance), **CloudTrail** (suspicious API calls), **CloudWatch** (metrics outside the norm).
- **Infrastructure protection**: **CloudFront** (first line of defense against DDoS), **Amazon VPC** (secure your network, correct ACLs), **Shield** (DDoS protection), **WAF** (Web Application Firewall), **Inspector** (security of EC2 instances).
- **Data protection**: **KMS** (encrypt data at rest), **S3** encryption mechanisms (**SSE-S3, SSE-KMS, SSE-C**, client-side encryption) and **bucket policies**; managed services provide their own protections — **Load Balancer HTTPS endpoints**, **EBS encryption at rest**, **RDS encryption at rest and SSL**.
- **Incident response**: **IAM** (delete or zero-privilege a compromised account), **CloudFormation** (recreate a deleted infrastructure), **CloudWatch Events** (automate alerting when a resource is deleted).

### Lecture: Pillar 3: Reliability

- **Definition**: the ability of a system to **recover from infrastructure or service disruptions**, **dynamically acquire computing resources to meet demand**, and **mitigate disruptions** such as **misconfigurations or transient network issues** — i.e., making sure the application runs no matter what.

**Design principles**
- **Test recovery procedures** — use automation to simulate failures or recreate scenarios that previously caused failures.
- **Automatically recover from failure** — anticipate and remediate failures **before they occur**.
- **Scale horizontally** to increase system availability and handle increased load.
- **Stop guessing capacity** — use **Auto Scaling** to have the right capacity at any time.
- **Manage change through automation** — so the application stays reliable and changes can be rolled back.

**AWS services mapped to the pillar**
- **Foundations**: **IAM** (nobody has excessive rights), **Amazon VPC** (strong networking foundation), **Service Limits** (set them appropriately — not too high, not too low — and **monitor them**; contact AWS to increase them as you grow to avoid service disruptions), **Trusted Advisor**.
- **Change management**: **Auto Scaling** (no manual change needed as the app grows), **CloudWatch** (watch metrics for databases and applications), **CloudTrail** (track API calls), **Config**.
- **Failure management**: **backups**, **CloudFormation** (recreate the whole infrastructure at once), **S3** (back up data), **S3 Glacier** (archives), and a **reliable highly available global DNS** — **Route 53** — which can be repointed to a new application stack for disaster recovery.

### Lecture: Pillar 4: Performance Efficiency

- **Definition**: the ability to **use computing resources efficiently to meet system requirements**, and to **maintain that efficiency as demand changes and technologies evolve** — adapting and providing the best performance.

**Design principles**
- **Democratize advanced technologies** — track new services as they become available; they may help your product development.
- **Go global in minutes** — deploying to multiple regions should take minutes, not days (e.g., with CloudFormation).
- **Use serverless architectures** — the "golden state": no servers to manage and everything scales for you.
- **Experiment more often** — something working today may not scale to 10× the load; try serverless architectures and see.
- **Mechanical sympathy** — be aware of all AWS services (courses, blogs); new changes can dramatically change your solution architecture.

**AWS services mapped to the pillar**
- **Selection**: **Auto Scaling**, **Lambda** (serverless), **EBS** (when you need a disk; manage performance via **gp2** or **io1**), **S3** (scale globally), **RDS** (provision a database, maybe migrate to **Aurora**) — many technologies scaling in different patterns; pick the right one.
- **Review**: **CloudFormation** (get exactly what you need before creating it) and the **AWS News Blog** to stay updated on performance improvements.
- **Monitoring**: **CloudWatch** (alarms, metrics, dashboards), and **Lambda** monitoring (avoid throttling, minimal execution time).
- **Trade-offs**:
  - **RDS vs. Aurora.**
  - **ElastiCache** — improve read performance, but risk **stale/outdated data** in the cache.
  - **Snowball** — moves lots of data very fast but takes about **a week** to arrive; trade-off between using your whole network capacity now vs. shipping the data.
  - **CloudFront** — go global in minutes, but content may be **cached for a day** on users' laptops, so updates take time to appear.

### Lecture: Pillar 5: Cost Optimization

- **Definition**: the ability to **run systems that deliver business value at the lowest price point**.

**Design principles**
- **Adopt a consumption model** — pay only for what you use. *Example*: **Lambda** — if you don't use it, you don't pay; **RDS** — you pay even when the database is idle because it is **provisioned**.
- **Measure overall efficiency** — use **CloudWatch**: are you using resources effectively?
- **Stop spending money on data center operations** — AWS handles the infrastructure so you focus on applications and systems.
- **Analyze and attribute expenditure** — **use tags** on AWS resources; without them it is very hard to know which application is costing money. Tags let you track and optimize per-application cost and compute the **ROI** relative to the revenue it generates.
- **Use managed application-level services to reduce cost of ownership** — managed services operate at **cloud scale** and therefore offer a **much lower cost per transaction**. *Example*: three AWS engineers running an application serving 5 million people, purely by leveraging the cloud.

**AWS services mapped to the pillar**
- **Expenditure awareness**: **Budgets**, **custom/usage reports**, **Cost Explorer**, **Reserved Instance reporting** (make sure reserved instances are actually being used).
- **Cost-effective resources**: **Spot Instances** (considerably cheaper, with trade-offs), **Reserved Instances** (for workloads running over 1–3 years, e.g., a database on EC2), **S3 Glacier** (the lowest price point for archives).
- **Matching supply and demand**: **Auto Scaling**, **Lambda** — avoid over-provisioning.
- **Optimizing over time**: **Trusted Advisor**, **Cost and Usage Report**, and reading the **News Blog**.
  - *Anecdote*: before ELB supported HTTP → HTTPS redirects natively, the instructor ran an extra application just to redirect; the feature announcement in the News Blog let him remove it and save money every month.
  - *Anecdote*: for a very low-activity **DynamoDB** application, the **on-demand** capacity mode is far better value than provisioned **reserved capacity (WCU/RCU)**.
- The exam will not ask deep questions on the framework, but understanding it is valuable as a solutions architect; reading the whitepaper is encouraged.

### Lecture: Pillar 6: Sustainability

- The **newest pillar**; it focuses on **minimizing the environmental impact of running cloud workloads**.

**Design principles**
- **Understand your impact** — establish **performance indicators**, evaluate improvements, ensure you reach **sustainability goals**, find the long-term ROI.
- **Maximize utilization** of your services — be **energy efficient** and environmentally conscious.
- **Anticipate and adopt new, more efficient hardware** over time — AWS optimizes its infrastructure, so using newer offerings is more efficient.
- **Use managed services where possible** — you **share infrastructure with many people**, which is better for sustainability.
- **Reduce the downstream impact of your cloud workloads** — reduce the energy/resources your services require and the need for customers to constantly upgrade their devices.

**AWS services that help with sustainability**
- **Compute**: **EC2 Auto Scaling**, serverless offerings (**Lambda**, **Fargate**), **Cost Explorer**, **Graviton 2**, **EC2 T-type instances**, and **Spot Instances** (use **spare capacity that would otherwise be wasted**) — use the **right amount of compute** for the task.
- **Storage tiering**: **EFS-IA**, **Amazon S3 Glacier**, **Cold HDD** EBS volumes — ask whether all your data really needs to be "hot".
- **Lifecycle**: **S3 Lifecycle Configurations**, **S3 Intelligent-Tiering**, **Amazon Data Lifecycle Manager** — keep data in the right tier.
- **Databases — "Read Local, Write Global"**: **RDS Read Replicas**, **Aurora Global Databases**, **DynamoDB Global Tables**, and **CloudFront**.

### Lecture: AWS Well-Architected Tool

- **Purpose**: **review your architectures against the six pillars** of the Well-Architected Framework and **adopt architectural best practices**.
- **How it works**: **select your workload** → **answer questions** → **review your answers against the six pillars** → **obtain advice** (videos, documentation, reports).

**Hands-on notes**
- **Define a workload**: name, description, **review owner**, **environment** (production, pre-production), **regions** you operate in (including non-AWS regions), **account IDs**, etc.
- **Apply lenses**: the **Well-Architected Framework lens**, the **FTR lens**, the **Serverless lens**, the **SaaS lens**, and others — plus your **own custom lens**.
- **Start reviewing**: answer questions covering all six pillars (**operational excellence, security, reliability, performance efficiency, cost optimization, sustainability**); you can mark a question as **not applicable** with a reason.
- **Save and exit** produces **identified risks** (**high risk, medium risk**, …) and lets you **save a milestone** (e.g., "first draft").
- The **Improvement Plan** lists **improvement items with links to documentation** based on your answers — helpful for teams to make sure applications are Well-Architected.

### Lecture: AWS Cloud Adoption Framework (CAF)

- **AWS CAF** is a **whitepaper / ebook**, **not a service**. It helps you **build and execute a comprehensive plan for your digital transformation** through innovative use of AWS.
- Created by AWS professionals from **best practices gathered across thousands of customers**.
- > **Exam Tip**: CAF comes up at the exam and **some parts must be remembered by heart**.

**Structure**
- **Organizational capabilities** that underpin successful cloud transformations, grouped into **six perspectives** — **you must remember the six perspectives**.

**The six perspectives**

| Category | Perspective | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Business capabilities** | **Business** | Ensures your **cloud investments accelerate your digital transformation ambitions and business outcomes** |
| | **People** | **The bridge between technology and business** (**important for the exam**); accelerates the cloud journey so organizations evolve toward a culture of **continuous growth and learning** where **change becomes business as usual**; focuses on **culture, organizational structure, leadership and workforce** |
| | **Governance** | **Orchestrates cloud initiatives** while **maximizing organizational benefits and minimizing transformation-related risks** |
| **Technical capabilities** | **Platform** | Helps build an **enterprise-grade, scalable, hybrid cloud platform**, modernize existing workloads and implement **new cloud-native solutions** |
| | **Security** | Achieves the **confidentiality, integrity and availability** of your data and cloud workloads |
| | **Operations** | Ensures **cloud services are delivered at a level that meets the needs of your business** |

**Capabilities within each perspective** *(you don't have to memorize them, but you may be asked to match a capability to its perspective — the names usually make it obvious)*
- **Business**: strategy management, portfolio management, innovation management, product management, strategic partnership, data monetization, business insight, data science.
- **People**: culture evolution, transformational leadership, cloud fluency, workforce transformation, change acceleration, organization design, organizational alignment.
- **Governance**: program and project management, benefits management, risk management, cloud financial management, application portfolio management, data governance, data curation.
- **Platform**: platform architecture, data architecture, platform engineering, data engineering, provisioning and orchestration, modern application development, continuous integration and continuous delivery.
- **Security**: security governance, security assurance, identity and access management, threat detection, vulnerability management, infrastructure protection, data protection, application security, incident response.
- **Operations**: observability, event management, incident and problem management, change and release management, performance and capacity management, configuration management, patch management, availability and continuity management, application management.

**The four transformation domains** (they help drive business outcomes; exam questions can target these)

| Domain | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Technology** | Use the cloud to **migrate and modernize** legacy infrastructure, applications, data and analytics platforms |
| **Process** | **Digitize, automate and optimize** business operations — leverage new data and analytics platforms to create **actionable insights**, and use **machine learning to improve customer service experience** |
| **Organization** | **Re-imagine your operating model** — **reorganize teams around products and value streams** and **leverage agile methods** to iterate and evolve rapidly (**this has been asked at the exam**) |
| **Products** | **Re-imagine your business model** by creating **new value propositions** — products, services and revenue models |

**The four transformation phases** (also examinable)

| Phase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **1. Envision** | Demonstrate how the cloud will **accelerate business outcomes** by **identifying transformation opportunities** and creating a foundation for digital transformation |
| **2. Align** | Look at the **six CAF perspectives**, **identify capability gaps**, and produce an **action plan** |
| **3. Launch** | **Build and deliver pilot initiatives in production** and demonstrate **incremental business value** |
| **4. Scale** | **Expand the pilot initiatives to the desired scale** while realizing the desired business benefits |

- > **Exam Tip**: expect to be asked to **identify which perspectives belong to the framework**, possibly to **match a capability to a perspective**, and about the **transformation domains and phases**. Much of it can be answered by **elimination and common sense**.

### Lecture: Right Sizing

- Because there are **so many instance types**, **choosing the most powerful one is not the best choice** — the cloud is **elastic** and you can change the instance type whenever you want.
- **Right sizing** = the process of **matching the instance type and size to your workload's performance and capacity requirements at the lowest possible cost**.
- Since you can **scale up easily** in the cloud, **always start small** to find the right size.
- It is also the process of **continuously reviewing all deployed instances** and their **metrics** to identify opportunities to **eliminate or downsize without compromising capacity or performance** — resulting in **lower cost**.

**When to right size (two moments)**
1. **Before a cloud migration** — it is very common for a company to migrate everything at the biggest instance size and forget about it. **Right size first.**
2. **Continuously after the migration** — e.g., **once a month**, because requirements change over time and you may need to size **up or down**.

- **Tools that help**: **CloudWatch**, **Cost Explorer**, **Trusted Advisor**, and third-party tools.
- > **Exam Tip**: the exam may ask **one question** about right sizing. Remember: **start small and scale up over time**, and right size **just before a cloud migration** and **continuously** afterwards.

### Lecture: AWS Ecosystem

**Free resources**
- **AWS Blog** — new feature releases and in-depth articles on specific use cases.
- **Community forums** — engage with other builders.
- **Whitepapers and guides** — in-depth learning on a specific area of AWS.
- **Solutions Library** (formerly **Quick Starts**) — **vetted technology solutions** for the cloud (e.g., "live streaming on AWS"). Each solution shows what it is, pros and cons, **architecture diagrams**, and a **launch button that takes you straight into CloudFormation** to create the stack.
- All of these are available **at no cost**.

**Support (as described in this lecture)**

| Level | What you get |
|---|---|
| **Developer** | **Business-hours email** access to **Cloud Support Associates**; **general guidance < 24 hours**; **system impaired < 12 hours** |
| **Business** | **24/7 phone, email and chat** access to **Cloud Support Engineers**; **production system impaired < 4 hours**; **production system down < 1 hour** |
| **Enterprise** | Most expensive tier — a **dedicated Technical Account Manager (TAM)**, a **concierge support team** for billing and account best practices; **business-critical system down < 15 minutes** |

**AWS Marketplace**
- A **digital catalog with thousands of software listings from independent (third-party) software vendors**.
- You can buy: a **custom AMI** (customized with an OS, firewalls or technical solutions), **production-ready CloudFormation templates**, **SaaS**, **containers**.
- **Advantage**: purchases go **directly onto your AWS bill**, so it's easy to source a new vendor.
- You can also **sell your own solutions** by becoming a **Marketplace seller**.

**Training**
- **AWS-delivered training**: **digital (online)** or **classroom (in-person or virtual)**; **private training** for your organization; dedicated **training and certification programs for the US government** and **for the enterprise**.
- **AWS Academy** — helps **universities teach AWS** so graduates know how to use the cloud.
- Third-party/online instructors (not sponsored by AWS) are another option.

**Professional Services and the Partner Network**
- **AWS Professional Services Organization** — a **global team of experts** that works **alongside your team** and a chosen member of the **APN**.
- **APN = AWS Partner Network**:
  - **APN Technology Partners** — provide **hardware, connectivity and software** (help on the **infrastructure** side).
  - **APN Consulting Partners** — **professional services firms** that help you **build on AWS**.
  - **APN Training Partners** — help you **learn AWS** and deliver AWS training.
  - **Competency Program** — awarded to APN partners who have **demonstrated technical proficiency and proven customer success** in specialized solution areas.
  - **Navigate Program** — helps **partners become better partners** (training the partners).
- > **Exam Tip**: expect **one or two questions** on finding the right ecosystem service/tool. The names are usually self-explanatory once you've seen them; revisit this lecture before the exam.

### Lecture: AWS re:Post

- **AWS re:Post** is a **community forum** where you **find answers, answer questions, find best practices and join groups**.
- It is an **AWS-managed Q&A service** offering **crowdsourced, expert-reviewed answers** to technical questions about AWS.
- It **replaces the original AWS forums**.
- Works like **Stack Overflow**: someone asks a question, answers can be **upvoted** or **accepted**, and are **reviewed by experts**.
- Community members **earn reputation** to build **expert status** and review other answers.
- If you are a **premium AWS customer** and the community doesn't respond, your question is **automatically passed to AWS Support engineers**, who will answer it.
- > **Exam Tip**: **re:Post is NOT for time-sensitive questions or anything involving proprietary action.** If you need help right now, re:Post is not the right service.

### Lecture: AWS Knowledge Center

- **AWS Knowledge Center** is **part of the re:Post portal**.
- It is where you find the **most frequent and common questions and requests** about AWS, organized in categories (popular services, analytics, application integration, …).
- Each entry has the **question** and a **resolution** with a long article (e.g., troubleshooting object replication for Amazon S3 buckets).
- You can browse **newly created** or **popular** content, **filter by tags or AWS service**, search, sort, and view as a list.
- > **Exam Tip**: Knowledge Center is the place to find answers to **very common questions — FAQs and best practices** on AWS.

### Lecture: AWS Managed Services

- **AWS Managed Services (AMS)** is **a team of AWS experts** — not a console service — that provides **infrastructure and application support on AWS**.
- What the AMS team does:
  - **Manages and operates your infrastructure** for **security, reliability and availability**.
  - Helps organizations **offload routine maintenance tasks** so they can focus on **business objectives**.
  - Handles common activities: **change requests, monitoring, patch management, security, and backup services**.
  - **Implements best practices** and maintains your infrastructure to **reduce operational overhead and risk**.
  - Runs **24/7, 365 days a year**.
- Helps you **create a baseline**, then **sustain, build or migrate**, and then **operate your infrastructure in the cloud**.
- **How to get it**: **not available from the console** — you must **contact AWS sales**, who will get in touch and help you.
- **Benefits**: **improved security**, a **focus on automation** (most of what they do is automated), **stronger compliance**, **reduced operating costs** (you deal with experts), **simplified management**, and **frictionless innovation**.

## Section: AWS Architecting & Ecosystem — Key Takeaways

**Most important concepts**
- The **Well-Architected Framework** has **six pillars**: **Operational Excellence, Security, Reliability, Performance Efficiency, Cost Optimization, Sustainability** — and they are a **synergy, not a set of trade-offs**.
- The framework's **guiding principles**: stop guessing capacity, **test at production scale**, automate experimentation (CloudFormation), allow **evolutionary architectures**, drive architecture **with data**, and improve through **game days** (Netflix's Chaos Monkey).
- The **cloud design best practices**: **scalability** (vertical/horizontal), **disposable resources**, **automation**, **loose coupling** (break the monolith; failures must not cascade), and **think in services, not servers**.
- Each pillar has its own definition and design principles worth knowing: **Operational Excellence** (operations as code, small reversible changes, learn from failure, observability), **Security** (strong identity foundation, traceability, security at all layers, automate, protect data in transit and at rest, keep people away from data, prepare for events), **Reliability** (test recovery, auto-recover, scale horizontally, stop guessing capacity, manage change through automation), **Performance Efficiency** (democratize advanced tech, go global in minutes, serverless, experiment, mechanical sympathy), **Cost Optimization** (consumption model, measure efficiency, stop paying for data centers, **attribute expenditure with tags**, use managed services), **Sustainability** (understand impact, maximize utilization, adopt efficient hardware, use managed services, reduce downstream impact).
- The **AWS Well-Architected Tool** operationalizes the framework: define a workload, apply **lenses**, answer questions, receive **risks** and an **improvement plan** with documentation links, and save **milestones**.
- The **Customer Carbon Footprint Tool** tracks, measures, reviews and forecasts **carbon emissions** from AWS usage, including the **path to 100% renewable energy**.
- The **Cloud Adoption Framework (CAF)** is a **whitepaper** with **six perspectives** (**Business, People, Governance | Platform, Security, Operations**), split into **business** and **technical** capabilities, plus **four transformation domains** (**Technology, Process, Organization, Products**) and **four transformation phases** (**Envision, Align, Launch, Scale**). **People is the bridge between technology and business.**
- **Right sizing** means matching instance type and size to the workload at the lowest cost; **start small**, right size **before migrating** and **continuously afterwards**, using **CloudWatch, Cost Explorer, Trusted Advisor**.
- The **AWS ecosystem** includes **free resources** (blog, forums, whitepapers, **Solutions Library / Quick Starts**), **support plans**, the **Marketplace** (third-party AMIs, CloudFormation templates, SaaS, containers — billed through AWS), **training** (digital, classroom, private, government, enterprise, **AWS Academy**), **Professional Services**, and the **APN** (**Technology, Consulting, Training partners**, plus the **Competency** and **Navigate** programs).
- **re:Post** is the community Q&A forum (expert-reviewed, reputation-based, escalates to AWS Support for premium customers, **not for time-sensitive issues**); **Knowledge Center** is the FAQ/best-practices part of the re:Post portal; **AWS Managed Services (AMS)** is a **team of AWS experts** (contacted through sales) that operates your infrastructure 24/7/365.

**How the concepts relate**
- The **six pillars are the vocabulary** used by the **Well-Architected Tool** and reflected across the whole course — CloudFormation, CloudWatch, IAM, Auto Scaling, Route 53 and Trusted Advisor each appear under multiple pillars, which is exactly the "synergy" the framework describes.
- **CloudFormation is the connective service** in this section: it appears in operational excellence (operations as code), security (incident recovery), reliability (recreate infrastructure), performance efficiency (go global in minutes), and it is also the delivery mechanism for **Solutions Library** and **Service Catalog** products.
- **Sustainability and Cost Optimization overlap heavily** — right sizing, Spot Instances, storage tiering, lifecycle policies and Graviton improve both bill and carbon footprint; the **Customer Carbon Footprint Tool** and **Cost Explorer** live in the same Billing console.
- **CAF vs. Well-Architected Framework**: CAF governs the **organizational journey to the cloud** (people, process, business case, phases), while the Well-Architected Framework governs the **technical quality of a workload** once you're there.
- **CAF's Align phase explicitly uses the six perspectives** to find capability gaps — the perspectives are not just a list, they are the assessment instrument.
- **The ecosystem supplies the human layer**: free resources and re:Post/Knowledge Center for self-service, support plans for reactive help, Professional Services and APN partners for project help, and **AMS** for fully outsourced operations.

**Key terminology to remember**
- Well-Architected Framework, six pillars, synergy (not trade-offs), game days, Chaos Monkey, disposable resources, loose coupling, monolith vs. microservices, "think in services, not servers"
- Operations as code, runbooks, observability, small reversible changes
- Least privilege, traceability, security at all layers, defense in depth, SSE-S3 / SSE-KMS / SSE-C, tokenization
- Test recovery procedures, automatic recovery, horizontal scaling, service limits

**Key distinctions/comparisons**
- **Well-Architected Framework vs. Cloud Adoption Framework**: technical best practices for **designing and reviewing workloads** (six pillars) vs. an **organizational transformation plan** (six perspectives, four domains, four phases).
- **Well-Architected Framework vs. Well-Architected Tool**: the **whitepaper/principles** vs. the **console service** that reviews a workload against them and produces risks and an improvement plan.
- **CAF business capabilities vs. technical capabilities**: **Business, People, Governance** vs. **Platform, Security, Operations**.
- **CAF transformation domains vs. phases**: **what** you transform (Technology, Process, Organization, Products) vs. **when/how you sequence it** (Envision → Align → Launch → Scale).
- **Monolith vs. loosely coupled components**: hard to maintain and scale, failures cascade vs. independent components linked by SNS/SQS where failures are contained.
- **Consumption-based vs. provisioned pricing**: Lambda (pay only when used) vs. RDS (pay while provisioned even when idle).
- **Reserved Instances vs. Spot Instances (cost pillar)**: predictable long-running workloads at a discount vs. much cheaper spare capacity with interruption risk.
- **Performance trade-offs**: ElastiCache (faster reads vs. possibly stale data), CloudFront (global speed vs. cached-content staleness), Snowball (huge throughput vs. ~a week of transit).
- **Right sizing before migration vs. continuously**: avoid the common mistake of lifting everything at the largest size vs. monthly review as requirements change.
- **Free resources vs. paid support vs. Professional Services vs. AMS**: self-service documentation and community vs. contractual response times vs. experts working alongside your team on a project vs. AWS operating your infrastructure for you 24/7.
- **re:Post vs. Knowledge Center vs. AWS Support**: crowdsourced expert-reviewed Q&A (**not time-sensitive**) vs. curated FAQ/best-practice articles vs. contractual support with response-time SLAs.
- **APN Technology vs. Consulting vs. Training partners**: hardware/connectivity/software vs. professional services firms that build on AWS vs. partners who deliver AWS training.
- **Support tiers (as described in this lecture)**: **Developer** (business-hours email, <24 h general guidance, <12 h system impaired) vs. **Business** (24/7 phone/email/chat, <4 h impaired, <1 h down) vs. **Enterprise** (dedicated **TAM**, concierge team, **<15 min** for business-critical down).
