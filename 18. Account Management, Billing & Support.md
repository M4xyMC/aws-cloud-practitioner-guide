## Section: Account Management, Billing & Support

### Lecture: Organizations Overview

- **AWS Organizations** is a **global service** that lets you **manage multiple AWS accounts**.
- The main account is the **Master Account** (also called the **Management Account**); all others are **Child Accounts**.

**Benefits (common exam material)**
- **Consolidated Billing** — all accounts are paid by the **Master Account**; you get **one bill** and don't need a payment method for the other accounts.
- **Pricing benefits from aggregated usage** — volume discounts (e.g., heavy EC2 or S3 usage) are calculated on the **combined** usage, so splitting workloads across accounts doesn't lose you the volume discount.
- **Reserved Instances are shared across all accounts** — if one account doesn't use its RI, another can, maximizing cost savings.
- **API to automate AWS account creation** — useful for programmatically creating e.g. **sandbox accounts**.
- **Restrict account privileges using Service Control Policies (SCP)** — a **common exam question**.

**Multi-account strategy**
- Create accounts **per department, per cost center, per environment** (dev/test/prod), or **based on regulatory restrictions**.
- Reasons to use multiple accounts:
  - **Regulatory restrictions** — block a service in an account with an **SCP**.
  - **Better resource isolation** — different VPCs in different accounts.
  - **Separate per-account service limits.**
  - **Isolated account for logging.**
- **Trade-off**: **multiple accounts** vs. **one account with multiple VPCs** (the instructor prefers multiple accounts).
- Best practices: use **tagging standards across all accounts** for billing; **enable CloudTrail on all accounts** and send logs to a **central S3 account**; send **CloudWatch Logs to a central logging account**.

**Organizational Units (OUs)**
- Accounts can be organized **by business unit** (e.g., Sales OU, Retail OU, Finance OU), **by environment** (production, development, test), **project-based** (Project-1, Project-2, …), or a **mix**.
- Structure: the **Root OU** contains everything, including the **Master Account**; you create OUs (e.g., Dev OU, Prod OU), and **OUs can be nested inside OUs** (e.g., Finance OU and HR OU inside the Prod OU), each with their own accounts.

**Service Control Policies (SCP)**
- **Whitelist or blacklist IAM actions**, applied at the **OU level or Account level**.
- **SCPs have NO effect on the Master Account.**
- They apply to **all Users and Roles of the account, including the account's root user** — e.g., if an SCP denies EC2, **even an admin in that account cannot use EC2**.
- SCPs do **not** apply to **service-linked roles** (the service roles other services use to integrate with Organizations).
- **SCPs must have an explicit Allow** — by default nothing is allowed.
- **Use cases (what the exam tests)**:
  - **Restrict access to certain services** — e.g., "in my production accounts, you cannot use EMR".
  - **Enforce PCI compliance** by explicitly disabling services that are not yet PCI-compliant in AWS.
- **Structure**: an SCP looks just like an IAM Policy. Examples:
  - **Blacklist**: `Allow *` on `*`, plus `Deny` on `dynamodb:*` for any resource → everything allowed **except** DynamoDB.
  - **Whitelist**: `Allow ec2:*` and `cloudwatch:*` on `Resource: *` → **only** EC2 and CloudWatch usable, nothing else.

### Lecture: Organizations Hands On

- Organizations is a **global service** (it concerns accounts and grouping them). The demo uses **two separate accounts**: a **master** and a **child**.
- **Create the organization** from the master account → the **Root organizational unit** is created containing the **master account** (a.k.a. the **management account**).
- **Adding an account — two options**:
  1. **Create an account** — specify the **account name**, the **email address of the account owner**, and an **IAM role** created in the target account so the organization can manage it.
  2. **Invite an existing AWS account** — provide the **email address** or the **account ID**. The invitation appears under **pending invitations** and **expires after two weeks** if not accepted.
- On the **child account**: `Organizations → Invitations` → the invitation states the organization has **full features enabled** and **can assume full control of your account** — accepting an invitation means **accepting to be controlled by the organization's master**. After acceptance the child account is enrolled and can only see the **organization ID** and **feature set**; an account **may have the ability to leave** the organization.
- **Creating OUs**: `Actions → Create new OU` on the root (e.g., **Dev**, **Test**, **Prod**), and **nested OUs** inside Prod (e.g., **HR**, **Finance**) — as many nested levels as you want.
- **Moving accounts**: select the account → **Move** → into the target OU (e.g., child account into Prod/Finance). **Best practice: leave the management account under the Root OU.**
- **Enabling policy types**: four policy types exist and are **disabled by default**. Enable **Service Control Policies** to restrict what child accounts can do. Others worth knowing:
  - **Backup policy** — deploy **organization-wide backup plans** so all accounts are compliant and have backups enabled.
  - **Tag policy** — **standardize tag usage** across all accounts in the organization.
  - (From an exam perspective **only SCPs** are used, but it's good to know backup and tag policies exist.)
- **The default SCP** is **`FullAWSAccess`**, which allows all accounts to access all services.
- **Creating an SCP**: e.g. `DenyAccessToS3` — statement with **effect Deny**, service **S3**, **all actions**, resource `*`.
- **Policy inheritance**: policies attached to Root are **inherited** down the tree — a nested account showed `FullAWSAccess` **four times** (attached directly, plus inherited from Finance, Prod and Root).
- **Attaching the SCP**: attach `DenyAccessToS3` to the **Finance OU** → the child account shows it as **inherited from Finance**.
- **Result**: logging into the child account and opening the S3 console gives **"you don't have permission to list buckets"** — S3 is unusable. This holds **even when logged in as that account's root user**, which is what makes SCPs so powerful.

### Lecture: Organizations Consolidated Billing

Enabling **Consolidated Billing** gives you **two things**:

**1. Combined usage**
- **Shared volume pricing** — e.g., in Amazon S3, once you pass a volume threshold (say 5 TB) the next tier is cheaper. If six accounts each use 1 TB, combining them means **all of them benefit from the discounted tier**.
- **Shared Reserved Instance and Savings Plan discounts** — discounts on one account can be **shared across all accounts** to maximize savings.

**2. One bill**
- **One bill for all accounts in the organization** — helps the accounting department and means you are **not restricted in how many AWS accounts you create**.

**Reserved Instance sharing — worked example**
- Organization with **Account A** (no RIs) and **Account B** (**5 reserved EC2 instances**). Reserved Instances are at the **AZ level**, so assume all within one AZ.
- **9 EC2 instances** are running: **3 in Account B**, **6 in Account A**.
- Account B's 3 instances are covered by its RIs. Because **RI sharing is enabled**, **2 instances in Account A also get Reserved Instance pricing**.
- **Result: 5 instances at RI pricing, 4 at On-Demand pricing** — even though Account B only launched 3 of its 5 reserved instances.
- > **Exam Tip**: the exam tests **shared volume discounts** and **what it means to share Reserved Instances**.
- **Reserved Instance discount sharing can be turned off** for any account in the organization, **including the management account**.

### Lecture: AWS Control Tower Overview

- **AWS Control Tower** is an **easy way to set up and govern a secure and compliant multi-account AWS environment based on best practices** — instead of manually creating an organization and applying security practices, you get a multi-account environment **in a few clicks**.
- **Benefits**:
  - **Automate the setup** of your environment in a few clicks.
  - **Automate ongoing policy management using guardrails.**
  - **Detect policy violations and remediate them.**
  - **Monitor compliance through an interactive dashboard.**
- **Control Tower runs on top of AWS Organizations** — it **automatically sets up Organizations** to organize your accounts and **implements SCPs** to make sure the guardrails operate effectively.

---

### Lecture: AWS Control Tower Hands On

- Not recommended to follow along: it creates **many accounts** and can be **very costly**.
- Setting up a **Landing Zone** involves:
  - **Home Region** — where Control Tower lives.
  - **Region deny setting** — deny access to specific regions.
  - **Additional regions for governance** — which regions to monitor.
  - **OUs** — a **Security OU** is created (for the **log archive** and **security audit** accounts) plus an additional **Sandbox OU**; more OUs can be created after setup.
  - **Accounts** — create the **log archive account** and the **audit account** (each needs an email).
  - **Account access configuration** — recommended: **IAM Identity Center**; self-managed account access is far more complicated.
  - **Enable CloudTrail** across the whole Landing Zone (better to enable), optional **logs to S3**, optional **KMS encryption**.
- Setup takes about **60 minutes** and creates: **2 OUs**, **3 shared accounts** (management, log archive, security audit), a **native cloud directory with preconfigured groups and single sign-on access**, **20 preventive guardrails** to enforce policies, and **2 detective guardrails** to detect configuration violations.
- Afterwards, **Organizations** shows the accounts in **core** (audit, archive) and **custom** OUs — but **you should manage accounts through Control Tower, not through Organizations**.
- The Control Tower **dashboard** shows non-compliant resources, registered OUs and their compliance, enrolled accounts, and the guardrails. Example guardrails: **disallow deletion of the log archive**, **disallow public read access to the log archive**, **disallow configuration changes to CloudTrail**.
- **Account Factory** is how you **enroll an account** into Control Tower. **Users and access** manages user access across the account set via **single sign-on** with a **user portal URL** — from the SSO portal you can log into any of the accounts (management console or programmatic/CLI access).
- Bottom line: if an organization wants a **multi-account best-practice AWS setup**, use **Control Tower**.

### Lecture: AWS Resource Access Manager (AWS RAM)

- **AWS RAM** lets you **share resources owned by your account with other AWS accounts** — any other account, or accounts within your organization.
- **Purpose: avoid resource duplication.**
- **Supported resources** include **Aurora databases, VPC Subnets, Transit Gateway** and many others (the exact list is not something to memorize — remember the idea behind RAM).
- **Example**: a cloud account owns a **VPC with private subnets** and shares it with **Account 1** and **Account 2**. Those accounts can then create **EC2 instances and load balancers inside the shared VPC**, and resources across accounts can talk to each other **directly at the network level** (e.g., Account 2's EC2 instances reaching an **RDS database** or **Application Load Balancer** created by another account) — which **simplifies deployments**.

### Lecture: AWS Service Catalog - Overview

- **Problem**: users new to AWS have **too many options**; if they can do anything, what they create **may not align with the rest of your organization**. Some users just want a **quick self-service portal** to launch a set of **authorized products predefined by admins** (virtual machines, databases, storage options, …).
- **AWS Service Catalog** provides that self-service portal.
- **How it works**:
  - As an **admin**, you create **products** — these are **CloudFormation templates with the proper parameters**.
  - Products are grouped into a **portfolio** (a **collection of products**).
  - You define **who has access to launch which products** in the portfolio.
  - As a **user**, you log into the **Service Catalog portal** and see the list of products your permissions allow, then **launch** them — they are **provisioned automatically by CloudFormation**.
- **Benefit**: the launched resources are **properly configured, properly tagged, and respect how your organization is supposed to work**.
- *Example*: a user wants a quick **RDS database** but doesn't know how to create one properly — offer it as a Service Catalog product.

### Lecture: Pricing Models of the Cloud

**AWS's four pricing models**
1. **Pay as you go** — pay for what you use; stay **agile** (start/stop/delete resources any time), **responsive**, and **scale to meet demand**.
2. **Save when you reserve** — **minimize risk**, get a **predictable budget**, comply with **long-term requirements**. Instance reservations exist for **EC2, DynamoDB, ElastiCache, RDS, Redshift**.
3. **Pay less by using more** — **volume-based discounts** (e.g., Amazon S3).
4. **Pay less as AWS grows** — as AWS's infrastructure grows they realize cost savings and **pass them on to you** (frequent price reductions from economies of scale).

**AWS Free Tier**
- A brand-new AWS account gets **up to $200 in credits**, then you choose between the **free plan** and the **paid plan**:

| Free plan | Paid plan |
|---|---|
| Expires in **6 months**, or when the **$200 in credits** are consumed | Account is **charged** after the credits are consumed |
| **No charges**, but the account is then **automatically deleted** | You **keep using** the account |

- Both plans include the **$200 credits** and access to **Always Free services** — monthly free usage limits regardless of plan, e.g.:
  - **Lambda**: **1 million requests/month** and **400,000 GB-seconds of compute/month**.
  - **DynamoDB**: **25 GB of storage** and **200 million requests/month**.

**EC2 pricing — what you pay for (On-Demand)**
- Number of **instances**; the **instance configuration** (capacity — number of CPUs and RAM); the **region**; the **operating system and software** installed; the **instance type and size**.
- For a **load balancer**: how long it runs and the **amount of data processed**.
- **Detailed monitoring** (CloudWatch metrics every 1 minute instead of every 5) is **paid**.

**EC2 purchasing models**

| Model | Discount / billing | Commitment & notes |
|---|---|---|
| **On-Demand** | Pay **per second** (Linux and Windows) or **per hour** (other OSes); **minimum billing time 60 seconds** | No commitment |
| **Reserved Instances** | Up to **75% discount** vs. On-Demand hourly rate | Commit for **1 or 3 years**; **more discount if you pay earlier** — **all upfront** > **partial upfront** > **no upfront** |
| **Spot Instances** | Up to **90% discount** vs. On-Demand hourly rate | **Bid for unused EC2 capacity**; you **risk losing them** if someone pays more |
| **Dedicated Hosts** | Available **On-Demand** or **Reserved** | You **run alone on the host** — it is dedicated to you |
| **Savings Plans** | (Covered in its own lecture) | An **alternative to all of the above** |

**Other compute pricing**
- **Lambda** — pay **per API call** and **per duration × amount of RAM assigned** to the function.
- **ECS (EC2 launch type)** — **no fee for ECS itself**; you pay for the **EC2 instances** underlying the cluster.
- **Fargate** — no EC2 instances to manage; pay **per container** for the **CPU and memory assigned**.

**S3 pricing**
- **Storage classes** to know: **S3 Standard, S3 Infrequent Access, S3 One-Zone IA, S3 Intelligent-Tiering, Glacier, Glacier Deep Archive**.
- Pay for the **number and size of objects**; prices are **tiered** — more/bigger objects means **more discount**.
- Pay for **requests** in and out of S3.
- Pay for **data transfer OUT of the S3 region** — **sending data into S3 is free**, retrieving costs money.
- **S3 Transfer Acceleration** is an extra cost.
- **Lifecycle transitions** between storage classes cost money.

**Other storage pricing**
- **EFS** — **pay per use**; also has an **Infrequent Access tier** and **lifecycle rules**.
- **EBS** — pay based on **volume type (performance)**, **size in GB provisioned in advance** (you pay for the provisioned size **whether you use it or not** — not pay-per-use), **IOPS** (included for general purpose SSD; **paid for provisioned IOPS**; number of requests for magnetic), **snapshots** (data cost per GB per month), and **data transfer out** (tiered volume discounts; **inbound writes are free**).

**RDS pricing**
- **Per-hour billing**, varying by **engine type, size, memory class**.
- **Purchase type**: **On-Demand** or **Reserved** (e.g., **1 or 3 years**, can pay upfront — a smart move).
- **Backups**: usually **no charge for backup storage up to 100% of your total database storage for a region** — in practice backup storage is effectively free unless the database is completely full.
- Also pay for: **underlying storage in GB/month** (the provisioned EBS volume), **number of I/O requests per month**, **deployment type** (**Single-AZ** vs. **Multi-AZ** — with Multi-AZ you pay for **two** RDS databases), and **data transfer** (**out is paid and tiered by volume; in is free**).

**CloudFront pricing**
- CloudFront is a **global CDN**, so pricing **differs by where content is served from** — usually **per continent/geography** (America, Europe, South America, Japan, Australia, India, …).
- **More usage in an edge location means more discounts**; you get an **aggregated bill for all edge locations**.
- Pay for **data transfer OUT** of CloudFront (**in is always free**) and for the **number of requests** made.

**Networking costs (simplified)**
| Traffic | Cost |
|---|---|
| **Inbound** to your EC2 instance | **Free** |
| Two EC2 instances **in the same AZ** using **private IP** | **Free** |
| Two EC2 instances in **different AZs** using **public IP** (traffic goes over the internet) | **$0.02 per GB** |
| Two EC2 instances in **different AZs** using **private IP** | **$0.01 per GB** |
| EC2 instance in **another region** | **$0.02 per GB** (inter-region cost) |

- **Summary**: **always use the private IP** where possible — **better savings and better network performance**. For maximum cost saving, use **one Availability Zone**, but you **sacrifice high availability** — a **trade-off between cost and high availability**.
- The exam won't test the exact per-service details, but it **will test your understanding of the reasoning behind AWS costs** at a high level.

### Lecture: Savings Plan Overview

- **Savings Plans** are a **simpler** way to save: instead of reserving instances, you **commit to spending a certain dollar amount per hour for 1 or 3 years**. You think in **dollars**, not in specific resources and types.

**Types of Savings Plans**

| Plan | Discount vs. On-Demand | Flexibility |
|---|---|---|
| **EC2 Savings Plan** | Up to **72%** | Commit to usage of **individual instance families in a region** (e.g., "$10/hour for 3 years on C5"), **regardless of AZ, size (C5.xl or C5.4xl), operating system (Linux/Windows), and tenancy (shared or dedicated host)** |
| **Compute Savings Plan** | Up to **66%** | **Most flexible** — regardless of **family, region, size, OS, tenancy, and compute option**. **Compute option** means it applies to **EC2 instances, Fargate containers AND Lambda functions** |
| **Machine Learning Savings Plan** | — | For services such as **SageMaker** |

- Like Reserved Instances, you can pay **all upfront, partial upfront, or no upfront** — **more upfront means a bigger discount**.
- **How to set one up**: go to the **AWS Cost Explorer** console — it **suggests the right Savings Plan** for your current infrastructure. Pricing can be estimated on the Savings Plan website.
- The Savings Plan pricing pages let you pick the **compute option** (EC2, Fargate, Lambda, EC2 Instances), **region**, **term length (1 or 3 years)**, and **payment option**, then list expected savings per instance type — e.g., **a1.large ≈ 31% saving**, **ml.t3.large SageMaker notebook ≈ 28% saving**.
- **Purchasing**: choose the Savings Plan type (**Compute** for Fargate/Lambda/EC2, **EC2 Instance**, or **SageMaker**), the **term commitment (1 or 3 years)**, the **hourly purchase commitment**, and the **payment option**; the console shows **upfront cost, monthly payment and total cost** before you add it to the cart.

### Lecture: Compute Optimizer Overview

- **AWS Compute Optimizer** **reduces costs and improves performance by recommending optimal AWS resources for your workloads**.
- Analyzes your **EC2 instances and Auto Scaling Groups** and tells you which are **over-provisioned or under-provisioned**, so you can optimize both **cost and performance**.
- **How**: uses **machine learning** to analyze **resource configuration** and track **CloudWatch metrics** to understand utilization.
- **Supported resources**: **EC2 instances, Auto Scaling Groups, EBS volumes, Lambda functions**.
- Can **lower costs by up to 25%** without much effort; **recommendations can be exported to Amazon S3**.

### Lecture: Estimating Costs in the Cloud - Pricing Calculator

- The **AWS Pricing Calculator** **estimates the cost of your designed solution architecture** — e.g., exactly how much it will cost over a year.
- **Workflow**: create an estimate → **select the services** you'll use → configure each one → add it to the estimate.
- **Example (EC2)**: select the **region**, choose a **quick or advanced estimate**, pick the **OS (Linux)**, specify the needed **vCPU and RAM** (e.g., 4 vCPU / 16 GB → **T4g.xlarge**), the **number of instances** (4), the expected **utilization per month** (80%), and the **pricing strategy** (e.g., **EC2 Instance Savings Plan, 1 year, no upfront**), plus **EBS storage per instance** (200 GB).
- **Example (Load balancer)**: add **Elastic Load Balancing** → **Application Load Balancer**, same region, quantity, **data processed per hour** (e.g., 5 GB/hour), **connections per second**, etc.
- The calculator then shows the **total 12-month cost** of the architecture (in the demo, about **$4,500**).
- It is **very granular**: multiple services, **groups**, and **support** can all be included.

### Lecture: Tracking Costs in the Cloud - Billing Dashboard, Cost Allocation Tags, Reports

**1. Billing Dashboard**
- Shows the **cost summary for the month**, the **forecast**, and **month-to-date** spend — a **very high-level tool** for an overview.
- Access: top-right → **Billing and Cost Management** (or search "billing").
- Provides a **cost breakdown by month**, **month-to-date cost**, **last month's cost**, **total forecasted** cost, and a **cost breakdown per service per month**. Costs can be **grouped by service, account, region**, etc., to drill down.

**2. Cost Allocation Tags**
- Let you **track costs at a detailed level and group them together**; you then **export a report** and get **cost by category** (e.g., in Excel format).
- **Two kinds of tags**:
  - **AWS-generated tags** — automatically applied to resources you create, prefixed **`aws:`** (e.g., `aws:createdBy`).
  - **User-defined tags** — created by the user, prefixed **`user:`**.
- Useful to group costs by **cost center, tag, owner, stack, application**, etc.
- **Tags are used for organizing resources** generally: EC2 instances, images, load balancers, security groups, RDS databases, VPC resources, Route 53, IAM users, etc. Resources created through **CloudFormation are all tagged the same way**.
- **Free naming** for tags; common tag names: **name, environment, team, cost center**.
- Tags can also be used to create **Resource Groups** — create, maintain and view a **collection of resources sharing common tags**; edit them with the **Tag Editor**.
- **Hands-on**: `Resource Groups & Tag Editor` → in **Tag Editor**, filter by resource type (e.g., EC2 security groups) → search resources → **Manage tags of selected resources** → add e.g. `department = IT` → review and apply. Then create a **tag-based Resource Group** (e.g., `department = IT`) → preview shows the tagged resources → name it and **Create group**.
- Finally, in the Billing console, **Cost Allocation Tags** must be **activated** before you can generate reports for a specific **user-defined cost allocation tag**.

**3. Cost and Usage Report**
- The **most comprehensive set of cost and usage data available on AWS**.
- Includes **additional metadata about services, pricing and reservations** (e.g., EC2 Reserved Instance usage).
- Lists **all usage for each service category used by an account and its IAM users** in **hourly or daily line items**, plus **any tags activated for cost allocation**.
- Can be **integrated and analyzed using Athena, Redshift, or QuickSight** (a dashboarding tool).
- It is the **most granular** report: for every cost it describes **when it was incurred, why it was incurred, and a description of the associated cost** — use it to understand exactly where a charge comes from.

**4. Cost Explorer**
- A **more visual tool** to **visualize, understand and manage your cost and usage over time**.
- Create **custom reports** analyzing cost and usage data — at a **high level**.
- View **total cost and usage across all accounts**, or **monthly, hourly, or at the resource level**.
- Shows your **optimal Savings Plan** to lower your bill (Savings Plan recommendations).
- > **Exam Tip**: Cost Explorer can **forecast usage up to 12 months** ahead based on previous usage — **this is an exam question**. If you need a tool to **forecast your bill 12 months ahead**, the answer is **Cost Explorer**.
- Examples of views: **monthly cost by AWS service**; **hourly and resource-level** cost (which EC2 instance costs most each hour); **Savings Plan recommendations**; **forecast** up to 12 months.

**Hands-on (Cost Analysis section)**
- **Data Exports** — export your **cost and usage reports**: create an export/dashboard → **standard data export** → export name → what to include → **time granularity** → **columns** → **delivery format (CSV or Parquet)** → overwrite or new file → destination **S3 bucket**. You can then analyze it with the tool of your choice or **Athena**.
- **Cost Explorer** — view costs over time by month with the biggest-spend services highlighted (e.g., Elastic Load Balancing), download the breakdown as **CSV**, change report parameters, and **save the report to the report library** for quick access.

### Lecture: Monitoring Costs in the Cloud - Billing Alarms & AWS Budgets

**Billing Alarms (CloudWatch)**
- The **billing metric is only stored in us-east-1** in CloudWatch.
- The data in us-east-1 is **aggregated for all AWS regions**.
- It corresponds to the **actual cost, not the projected cost**.
- You create a **billing alarm** on the billing metric — a **simple alarm**, **not as powerful as Budgets**, useful to send an **email notification** when you cross a threshold (e.g., threshold set to $70 → email when spend crosses $70).

**AWS Budgets**
- Send **alarms when cost exceeds the budget**, or when the **forecast** exceeds the budget.
- **Four types of budget**: **usage, cost, reservation, savings plan**.
- For **reserved instances** it can **track utilization**; supported for **EC2, ElastiCache, RDS and Redshift** Reserved Instances.
- Supports **up to 5 SNS notifications per budget** — can send emails, trigger Lambda functions, etc.
- Can **filter by**: service, linked account, tag, purchase option, instance type, region, AZ, API operation, etc. — **the same options as AWS Cost Explorer**.

**Hands-on notes**
- The Budgets console shows existing budgets with the **budgeted amount, amount used, forecasted amount**, and current vs. budgeted comparison.
- Creating a budget: use a **template** or **customize** → choose one of the four types (**cost, usage, savings plan, reservation**).
- For a **cost budget**: name it → set the **period** (**monthly, daily, quarterly, annually**), **recurring or expiring**, start/end dates → set a **fixed amount** (e.g., $10) → set the **budget scope**: **all AWS services** or **filter specific AWS cost dimensions** (e.g., only EC2, or Key Management Service).
- **Budget alerts**: add **alert thresholds** — e.g., at **80% of the actual budgeted amount** send an email, and at **80% of the forecasted amount** send an email. The graph shows where the thresholds and current spend lie.
- Budgets and **Cost Explorer are linked** — you can drill into the costs in Cost Explorer.

### Lecture: AWS Cost Anomaly Detection

- **AWS Cost Anomaly Detection** **continuously monitors your cost and usage data** and uses **machine learning to detect unusual spends**.
- It **learns your unique historical patterns** and detects **one-time cost spikes** and/or **continuous cost increases**.
- **You don't define anything — no thresholds required**; it determines on its own what looks unusual.
- **Monitors**: your **AWS services**, **member accounts**, **cost allocation tags**, and **cost categories**.
- Sends an **anomaly detection report with root cause analysis** of what is happening in your account.
- **Notifications**: **individual alerts** or a **daily or weekly summary**, leveraging **SNS**.

### Lecture: AWS Service Quotas

- AWS has **limits**, called **quotas**, and you may **hit them** (e.g., how many Lambda functions can run concurrently).
- **AWS Service Quotas** lets you:
  - **Create CloudWatch Alarms directly in the Service Quotas console** to be alerted when you approach a quota (e.g., Lambda concurrent executions reaching the quota).
  - **Request a quota increase** directly from the console — or decide to **shut down resources** if you discover too many things are running.
- Summary: Service Quotas **monitors all your quotas across AWS**, provides **alerting via CloudWatch Alarms**, and allows **quota increase requests** from the console.

### Lecture: AWS Trusted Advisor

- **AWS Trusted Advisor** requires **no installation** — it gives a **high-level account assessment** with **checks and advice**.
- Example checks: **EBS Public Snapshots**, **RDS Public Snapshots**, **are you using the root account?**
- **Six categories of checks**: **cost optimization, performance, security, fault tolerance, service limits, operational excellence**.
- There is a **core set of checks** and a **full set of checks**:
  - The **full set of checks requires a Business or Enterprise support plan**.
  - With Business/Enterprise support you also get **programmatic access to Trusted Advisor through the AWS Support API**.
- **Hands-on observations**: the dashboard shows **recommended actions and investigations** (e.g., a bucket allowing **global access**; **29 of 60 security group rules allow unrestricted access to a specific port**). Without a paid support plan, **Cost optimization, Performance, Fault tolerance and Operational excellence checks are unavailable** — only the **core security checks** (bucket permissions, security group ports, EBS public snapshots, RDS public snapshots) and **Service limits** are accessible; advanced security checks also require a support plan upgrade.
- **Service limits** can be viewed in Trusted Advisor (e.g., Auto Scaling Groups, CloudFormation stacks, DynamoDB read/write capacity).

## Section: Account Management, Billing & Support — Key Takeaways

**Most important concepts**
- **AWS Organizations** (global) groups a **management/master account** with **child accounts** into **OUs** (nestable), giving **consolidated billing**, **aggregated volume discounts**, **Reserved Instance/Savings Plan sharing**, an **account-creation API**, and **SCPs** for restricting privileges.
- **SCPs** whitelist/blacklist IAM actions at the **OU or account** level, apply to **all users and roles including the account root user**, have **no effect on the management account**, don't apply to **service-linked roles**, and require an **explicit Allow**.
- **AWS Control Tower** automates a **best-practice multi-account landing zone** on top of Organizations, with **preventive and detective guardrails**, a **log archive** and **audit** account, and **IAM Identity Center** single sign-on.
- **AWS RAM** shares resources (VPC subnets, Aurora DBs, Transit Gateway, …) **across accounts** to avoid duplication; **AWS Service Catalog** gives users a **self-service portal of admin-approved CloudFormation products** organized into **portfolios**.
- **Four pricing models**: **pay as you go**, **save when you reserve**, **pay less by using more (volume discounts)**, **pay less as AWS grows**.
- **EC2 purchasing options**: **On-Demand** (per second, 60-second minimum), **Reserved** (up to **75%** off, 1 or 3 years), **Spot** (up to **90%** off, can be lost), **Dedicated Hosts**, and **Savings Plans**.
- **Savings Plans** commit **dollars per hour** for 1 or 3 years: **EC2 Savings Plan (up to 72%, instance family in a region)**, **Compute Savings Plan (up to 66%, most flexible — EC2 + Fargate + Lambda)**, and **Machine Learning Savings Plan (SageMaker)**.
- **Cost tooling maps to three jobs**: **estimate** (Pricing Calculator), **track** (Billing Dashboard, Cost Allocation Tags, Cost and Usage Report, Cost Explorer), **monitor** (Billing Alarms, Budgets) — with **Cost Anomaly Detection** (ML-based), **Compute Optimizer** (right-sizing) and **Service Quotas** (limits) alongside.
- Data-transfer economics: **inbound is free almost everywhere**; **outbound and cross-AZ/cross-region traffic costs money**; **private IP is cheaper than public IP**.
- **Support plans** ladder from **Basic (free, 7 Trusted Advisor checks)** → **Business Support+ (full Trusted Advisor + API, 24/7 engineers, <30 min critical response, third-party software support)** → **Enterprise (TAM, <15 min response, Security Incident Response team, AWS Countdown)** → **Unified Operations (architecture guidance, full specialist team, Countdown Premium, CIRT)**.
- **Trusted Advisor** checks six categories (**cost optimization, performance, security, fault tolerance, service limits, operational excellence**); the **full set of checks requires Business or Enterprise support**.

**How the concepts relate**
- **Organizations is the foundation**: Control Tower builds on it, SCPs are enforced through it, consolidated billing and RI/volume sharing come from it, and Firewall Manager (from the Security section) also relies on it.
- **Tags are the connective tissue of billing**: you tag resources → activate them as **cost allocation tags** → they show up in the **Cost and Usage Report** and can be filtered in **Cost Explorer** and **Budgets**, and they can also be grouped into **Resource Groups**.
- **The billing metric → billing alarm** chain is the same CloudWatch mechanism from the monitoring section, and explains why **billing alarms only exist in us-east-1**; **Budgets** is the more capable alternative (forecast-aware, 4 budget types, filters, up to 5 SNS notifications).
- **Cost Explorer is the hub for savings**: it forecasts up to **12 months** and **recommends Savings Plans**, while **Compute Optimizer** recommends right-sizing and **Cost Anomaly Detection** catches unexpected spikes.
- **Support plan level gates other services**: full **Trusted Advisor** checks and its **API** are unlocked by Business/Enterprise support, and only the root user can **change or cancel the support plan** (from the Security section).
- **Service Catalog + CloudFormation + Organizations/Control Tower** together implement governance: standardized, pre-approved, correctly tagged resources deployed consistently across accounts.

**Key terminology to remember**
- Organizations, management/master account, child account, Root OU, Organizational Unit (OU), nested OU, service-linked role
- Service Control Policy (SCP), whitelist vs. blacklist, explicit Allow, `FullAWSAccess`, backup policy, tag policy
- Consolidated billing, combined usage, shared volume pricing, Reserved Instance sharing (AZ level)
- Control Tower, Landing Zone, home region, region deny, guardrails (preventive vs. detective), Account Factory, log archive account, audit account, IAM Identity Center / single sign-on
- AWS RAM (Resource Access Manager); AWS Service Catalog, product, portfolio
- Pay as you go, reserve, volume discounts, Free Tier ($200 credits, free vs. paid plan, Always Free services)
- On-Demand, Reserved Instances (1/3 years; all/partial/no upfront), Spot Instances, Dedicated Hosts, Savings Plans (EC2, Compute, Machine Learning), tenancy
- Storage classes, lifecycle transition, provisioned IOPS, snapshot, Single-AZ vs. Multi-AZ
- Pricing Calculator, Billing Dashboard, cost allocation tags (`aws:` vs. `user:`), Resource Groups, Tag Editor, Cost and Usage Report, Cost Explorer, Data Exports
- Billing metric (us-east-1, actual not projected), billing alarm, AWS Budgets (usage/cost/reservation/savings plan), budget alert threshold
- Cost Anomaly Detection, root cause analysis; Compute Optimizer; Service Quotas
- Trusted Advisor (core vs. full checks, six categories, AWS Support API), TAM, DSE, SBAS, IME, SSE, CIRT, AWS Countdown

**Key distinctions/comparisons**
- **Organizations vs. Control Tower**: the underlying multi-account/billing/SCP service vs. the **automated best-practice setup and governance layer** built on top of it.
- **Multiple accounts vs. one account with multiple VPCs**: stronger isolation, separate service limits, SCP-based restrictions vs. simpler but coarser separation.
- **SCP vs. IAM policy**: same syntax, but SCPs apply to **entire accounts/OUs including their root users** and act as a **boundary**, not a grant (they need an explicit Allow and don't affect the management account).
- **On-Demand vs. Reserved vs. Spot vs. Dedicated Hosts**: no commitment/pay per second vs. up to 75% off for 1–3 year commitment vs. up to 90% off but interruptible vs. dedicated, isolated hardware.
- **EC2 Savings Plan vs. Compute Savings Plan**: up to **72%**, locked to an **instance family in a region** vs. up to **66%**, flexible across **family, region, size, OS, tenancy** and covering **EC2, Fargate and Lambda**.
- **Reserved Instances vs. Savings Plans**: reserve specific instance capacity/types vs. commit a **dollar amount per hour** (simpler to reason about).
- **Free plan vs. paid plan (new accounts)**: expires after 6 months or when credits are exhausted and the **account is deleted** vs. charged after credits and the account continues.
- **EBS vs. EFS/S3 pricing**: pay for **provisioned** capacity whether used or not vs. **pay per use**.
- **Data transfer in vs. out**: **in is free** (S3, EBS, RDS, CloudFront) vs. **out is paid and tiered**.
- **Private IP vs. public IP for cross-AZ traffic**: **$0.01/GB** vs. **$0.02/GB** (public also traverses the internet).
- **Billing Dashboard vs. Cost Explorer vs. Cost and Usage Report**: high-level monthly overview vs. visual analysis with **12-month forecasting** and Savings Plan recommendations vs. the **most granular and comprehensive** raw data set (analyzable with Athena/Redshift/QuickSight).
- **Billing Alarms vs. AWS Budgets**: simple CloudWatch alarm on **actual** cost in **us-east-1** vs. four budget types, **forecast-based** alerts, rich filters, up to **5 SNS notifications** per budget.
- **Budgets vs. Cost Anomaly Detection**: you define thresholds vs. **machine learning detects unusual spend with no thresholds defined**.
- **Compute Optimizer vs. Cost Explorer**: right-size resources (over/under-provisioned) using ML and CloudWatch metrics vs. visualize, forecast and plan spend.
- **Trusted Advisor core vs. full checks**: limited (security, service limits) on Basic vs. all six categories plus **Support API access** on Business/Enterprise.
- **Business Support+ vs. Enterprise**: <30-minute critical response, no TAM vs. <15-minute response, **designated TAM**, Security Incident Response team, business reviews, AWS Countdown.
